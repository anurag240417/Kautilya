# Ethics, limits and false-positive risk

Use this as the content for the ethics / limitations slide and for judge questions.

## Design commitments

1. **Triage, not verdicts.** Scores rank leads for a human analyst. Every screen and report carries a disclaimer.
2. **Observation / inference / prediction are labelled.** A fact in the data, a published heuristic, and a model output are never
   shown as one another.
3. **No blanket accusations.** An entity is a *wallet fragment* (addresses probably under common control), never a named person.
   IP correlation is always shown with its uncertainty and a "correlation is not attribution" note.
4. **Uncertainty is visible.** Probabilities are calibrated and shown with a 90% range; the wallet-to-IP link shows a 95% interval.
5. **Explainable by construction.** Every flag lists the factors that raised or lowered it, with supporting TXIDs.
6. **Tamper-evident reports.** SHA-256 over the evidence, the dataset fingerprint, and the model/config hash; verifiable offline.
7. **Humans correct the system.** Confirm / false-positive verdicts feed retraining, and the active-learning list asks for the most useful labels first.
8. **Offline and minimal.** No outbound network calls; case data stays in a local SQLite file.

## Where false positives come from (and what we did)

| Benign behaviour | Looks like | Mitigation in the system |
|---|---|---|
| Exchanges batch-paying withdrawals | fan-out / layering | Service-like hubs are flagged and not expanded in the graph; batch payouts are reported as evidence but not scored |
| Exchanges consolidating deposits | darknet consolidation | Consolidation has very low weight (0.08) |
| Payment processors forwarding in minutes | rapid-hop layering | Needs a *chain* of three or more single-output hops, not one forward |
| Privacy users on Tor, CoinJoin participants | obfuscation | CoinJoin alone adds only 0.12; network signal is capped and fused, never decisive alone |
| Legitimate users with slow peel-like change spending | peel chains | Peel links require hops within 12 hours |
| Unusual but innocent entities | anomaly | Anomaly-only alerts are capped (uncorroborated cap 60/100) |

## Limitations (say these out loud)

* **All evaluation data is synthetic** and generated by us. The heuristics target patterns the generator plants. Results show the
  method works on modelled typologies, not real-world accuracy. A real deployment needs labelled real cases.
* **In-distribution accuracy is easy to get:** plain logistic regression matches our Random Forest. The meaningful tests are the
  unseen-scenario ones, where the darknet-market typology is the weakest (PR-AUC about 0.63 to 0.79 depending on the detector).
* **On real labelled data (Elliptic++) only the supervised model helps:** PR-AUC 0.79, while rules and anomaly detection score
  below chance and fusing them lowers accuracy. Performance also collapses in the final time window (PR-AUC 0.32) after the
  dark-market shutdown, so models need retraining and drift monitoring. See `reports/BENCHMARK.md`.
* **Entity resolution is pure but incomplete** (~8 fragments per multi-address owner in our synthetic data). Receive-only
  addresses cannot be linked.
* **Graph embeddings added little** beyond neighbour propagation in our ablation; we do not claim otherwise.
* **The bundled Tor/VPN lists and default GeoIP table are samples.** Real data must be dropped in (see `docs/GEOIP_SETUP.md`).
* **Adversaries adapt.** Slower layering, larger peel gaps, and mixing with legitimate traffic will reduce recall. Robustness tests
  cover noise and missing features, not a deliberate evasion strategy.
* **Not evidence for court on its own.** The report supports an investigation; independent verification is required.
* **The 1M-transaction scale claim is extrapolated,** not measured (see `reports/SCALE_TEST.md`).
